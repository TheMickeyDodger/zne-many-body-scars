## 1. Scientific question

Does zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE), applied to a first-order Trotter simulation of quantum many-body scar (QMBS) dynamics in the one-dimensional mixed-field Ising model, reduce the error of the staggered-magnetization observable relative to the noiseless value of the *same* circuit, under a fully specified local depolarizing noise model in a classical simulator? The deliverable is a **measurement** of whether, and by how much, ZNE improves (or degrades) the observable at each Trotter step and on average over the evolution window — not a claim that it will. The question is falsifiable: the design pre-registers (§13) an improvement-factor metric and a pass/fail criterion, and commits to reporting a negative result (improvement factor ≤ 1) unmodified. Everything runs locally in `qiskit-aer`; no hardware claims are made (§14).

**JUSTIFICATION:** the Paper demonstrates error-mitigated scar dynamics on IBM hardware and the Example reproduces a simplified version with Mitiq on a depolarizing-noise simulator; neither isolates ZNE's contribution under a *controlled, exactly known* noise channel with a deterministic noiseless reference. A local density-matrix study can do exactly that, which is the niche of this repository.

## 2. Physical system

The system is the one-dimensional **mixed-field Ising model (MFIM)** on an open chain of $L$ spin-1/2 sites — named exactly this way ("mixed-field Ising model (MFIM)") in both the Paper (Sec. I.A) and the Example. It is **not** the PXP model, but the two are directly related, and the sources discuss the relationship: the Paper derives the MFIM from a Rydberg-atom Hamiltonian $H = 4V\sum_{i=1}^{L-1} n_i n_{i+1} + \Omega \sum_{i=1}^{L} X_i$ with $n_i = (I - Z_i)/2$, and states that in the Rydberg-blockade regime $V \gg \Omega$ nearest-neighbor $|11\rangle$ configurations are suppressed, the accessible states form the Fibonacci Hilbert space, and "an effective model for the system in this limit is known as the 'PXP model', which arises from the projection of $H_X$ into the Fibonacci Hilbert space" (Paper, Sec. I.A). The regime studied here, $V/\Omega \simeq 4.17$ (Paper, Sec. III), is in the blockade regime but the simulation is of the full MFIM, not the projected PXP model.

**JUSTIFICATION:** using the MFIM exactly as the sources define it makes the local study directly comparable to both the Paper's hardware data and the Example's simulator data.

## 3. Hamiltonian

Exactly as written in the Paper, Eq. (1.2) (equivalent to Eq. (1.1) up to an additive constant):

$$
H = H_{ZZ} + H_Z + H_X
= V\sum_{i=1}^{L-1} Z_i Z_{i+1}
\;-\; 2V\sum_{i=2}^{L-1} Z_i
\;-\; V\,(Z_1 + Z_L)
\;+\; \Omega\sum_{i=1}^{L} X_i ,
$$

where $Z_i, X_i$ are Pauli operators on site $i \in \{1,\dots,L\}$.

- **Boundary conditions:** open. The Paper states explicitly: "the model is written with open boundary conditions, and … the longitudinal field strength is reduced by a factor of two on the first and last sites of the chain ($i=1,L$)" (Paper, Sec. I.A). Hence the boundary term $-V(Z_1+Z_L)$ versus the bulk term $-2V Z_i$; both are written out above and both must be implemented.
- **Parameter values:** $V = 1$, $\Omega = 0.24$, and Trotter step $\Delta t = 1$ (in units where $\hbar = 1$ and energies are measured in units of $V$). Source: the Paper states "After trying many sets of parameters, we settled on $V=1$, $\Omega=0.24$, and $\Delta t = 1$ as optimal parameters to simulate the system's dynamics in the QMBS regime" (Sec. II, end); the Example uses the same values ($V=1$, $\Omega=0.24$, $dt=1$). The ratio $V/\Omega \simeq 4.17$ places the model in the QMBS (blockade) regime $V \gg \Omega$ (Paper, Secs. I.A, III).
- The longitudinal-field strength is tied to the interaction strength $V$ "as a consequence of the model's origin" in the Rydberg Hamiltonian (Paper, Sec. I.A) — it is not an independent parameter.

**JUSTIFICATION:** every coefficient is taken verbatim from the Paper; there are no free parameters in the Hamiltonian for this study.

## 4. Initial state

The **Néel state** $|Z_2\rangle = |010101\rangle$ for the minimal experiment at $L=6$ (Paper notation: $|Z_2\rangle = |010\ldots\rangle$; the Example says "Néel state, that is the $|010101\rangle$ state"). Computational-basis labels follow the sources: $|0\rangle_i, |1\rangle_i$ are eigenstates of $Z_i$ with $n_i|0\rangle_i = 0$, $n_i|1\rangle_i = |1\rangle_i$, i.e. $Z_i|0\rangle_i = +|0\rangle_i$ (Paper, Sec. I.A).

**Qubit-ordering convention (binding for all M2 code):** physical site $i \in \{1,\dots,L\}$ maps to Qiskit qubit $q_{i-1}$; site 1 is Qiskit qubit $q_0$. Two ket notations are in play and are never interchangeable:

$$
|s_1 s_2 \ldots s_6\rangle_{\text{site}} = |010101\rangle
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
|q_5 q_4 \ldots q_0\rangle_{\text{Qiskit}} = |101010\rangle .
$$

Every ket in this document is **site-ordered (site 1 leftmost)** unless subscripted "Qiskit". This convention is anchored in the Paper itself: the Fig. 1 caption states the circuit "prepares the Néel state $|01010\rangle$ from the polarized state $|00000\rangle$ using $X$ gates" for $L=5$, i.e. site-1-leftmost kets. Qiskit, by contrast, displays and orders bitstrings little-endian, $q_{L-1}\ldots q_0$ with $q_0$ rightmost, so the same physical state carries the *reversed* Qiskit label. Concretely: the state is prepared from $|0\rangle^{\otimes L}$ by applying $X$ to qubits $q_1, q_3, q_5$ (sites 2, 4, 6, matching the Paper's Fig. 1 X-gate placement); in a measured count string `b[5]…b[1]b[0]`, site $i$ is bit `b[i-1]`, so **site 1 is the rightmost bit** — the Example's own analysis code reverses the count-string index for exactly this reason. All M2 observable code must consume count strings through a single site-indexing helper (§17, `observables.py`) so the reversal lives in one audited place.

**JUSTIFICATION (why this state probes scars):** the Paper explains that exact simulations of a quench from $|Z_2\rangle$ show weakly damped coherent oscillations of $\langle Z_\pi(t)\rangle$ with frequency $\omega \simeq 1.33\,\Omega$, arising from a tower of scarred eigenstates with roughly equal energy spacings that have high overlap with the Néel states, whereas the eigenstate-thermalization hypothesis would predict rapid decay to the thermal value 0 on a timescale $\sim 1/\Omega$ (Paper, Sec. I.B). The Néel state is therefore precisely the initial state whose dynamics distinguishes scarring from thermalization.

## 5. Observable(s)

**Candidates** (all three are measured in the Paper, Sec. I.B):

1. Staggered magnetization $Z_\pi = \sum_{i=1}^{L} (-1)^i Z_i$ (Paper Eq. (1.4)).
2. Loschmidt echo $\mathcal{L}(t) = |\langle \psi(t)|\psi(0)\rangle|^2$ with $|\psi(0)\rangle = |Z_2\rangle$ (Paper Eq. (1.5)).
3. Connected unequal-time correlation function $\mathcal{C}_Y(t) = \langle Y_\pi(t) Y_\pi(0)\rangle$ (Paper Eq. (1.6a)).

**Primary observable (chosen): the staggered-magnetization density**

$$
\frac{\langle Z_\pi(t)\rangle}{L}, \qquad Z_\pi = \sum_{i=1}^{L} (-1)^i Z_i .
$$

Normalisation: division by $L$, so the observable lies in $[-1, 1]$; it takes the extremal values $\langle Z_\pi \rangle = \pm L$ (density $\pm 1$) on the Néel states $|Z_2'\rangle$ and $|Z_2\rangle$ respectively (Paper, Sec. I.B). With the sign convention above ($Z|0\rangle = +|0\rangle$, site 1 in $|0\rangle$ contributing $(-1)^1 Z_1 = -1$), the initial value on $|Z_2\rangle$ is $\langle Z_\pi\rangle/L = -1$, consistent with the Paper's Fig. 3, where $Z_\pi/L$ starts at $-1$.

**This is the observable the sources actually use as their headline quantity:** the Paper's Figs. 3–4 show $\langle Z_\pi(t)\rangle/L$, and the Example measures the "staggered z-magnetization" $Z_\pi = \sum_i (-1)^i Z_i$. No deviation.

**JUSTIFICATION of the choice:** (a) fidelity to the sources — it is the quantity for which both the Paper and the Example report ZNE-mitigated results, so our simulation-only numbers are directly comparable; (b) it is a sum of single-qubit $Z$ operators, measurable in a single computational-basis measurement setting, keeping the minimal experiment to one circuit per time point; (c) it is traceless, so its value in the maximally mixed state is exactly 0 — this motivates the fixed asymptote used by the secondary extrapolation model in §11 (a statement about the fully-depolarized limit only, not a proof of the shape of the noise response; see §11). The Loschmidt echo is rejected for the minimal experiment because it is exponentially sensitive in system size and probes a single projector amplitude (Paper, Sec. IV.A, notes its "exponential sensitivity"); $\mathcal{C}_Y$ is rejected because it requires $4 \times 2 \times \lfloor L/2\rfloor$ auxiliary circuits per time point (Paper, Sec. IV.B). Both are natural M3 extensions.

## 6. Time evolution

**Scheme:** first-order Trotter (Lie–Trotter) decomposition, exactly the Paper's Eq. (1.3):

$$
U(\Delta t) \simeq e^{-i H_{ZZ}\,\Delta t}\; e^{-i H_Z\,\Delta t}\; e^{-i H_X\,\Delta t},
\qquad
U(n\,\Delta t) \simeq \bigl[e^{-i H_{ZZ}\Delta t} e^{-i H_Z\Delta t} e^{-i H_X\Delta t}\bigr]^n .
$$

All three factors are products of mutually commuting terms within each factor ($H_{ZZ}$ and $H_Z$ are diagonal; $H_X$ is a sum of single-site $X$ terms), so each factor is implemented exactly by the gate layers of §7.

- **Step size:** $\Delta t = 1$ (with $V=1$, the dimensionless time $Vt$ equals the number of Trotter steps; Paper, Sec. II end).
- **Number of steps:** $n = 1, \dots, 40$; **total evolved time** $T = 40/V$, covering about two scar-oscillation periods ($2\pi/(1.33\,\Omega) \simeq 19.68$; Paper, Sec. II end). The Paper's hardware runs use up to 39 steps (v2 abstract: "up to 39 Trotter steps"); the Example uses `n_dt = 40`. We adopt 40 to match the Example's grid. (The arXiv abstract page for the listing says "40 Trotter steps" while the v2 PDF abstract says 39 — a v1/v2 discrepancy in the sources themselves; both were fetched, we note it and move on.)
- **Trotter error:** for first-order Trotter the per-step error is $O(\Delta t^2)$, and the Paper states the leading-order scaling for this model: "the Trotter error scales to leading order as $V\Omega(\Delta t)^2$" (Sec. II, end). At $\Delta t = 1$ this is deliberately *not* small; the Paper acknowledges "the sizable value of $\Delta t$ incurs substantial Trotter error" and even notes the circuit can be viewed as a Floquet system (Paper Eq. (2.1)) in which QMBS are established phenomena.
- **Separability of error sources:** Trotter error is a **systematic, noise-independent** discretization error of the ideal circuit; it is *not* what ZNE targets. ZNE targets the gap between the noisy and noiseless expectation values **of the same circuit**. The design keeps the two separable by construction: the primary comparison target (§12) is the noiseless statevector simulation of the *Trotterized* circuit, so Trotter error cancels identically out of every ZNE metric in §13. The continuous-time exact solution is recorded separately, and the difference between the two references *is* the Trotter error, reported as its own curve.

**JUSTIFICATION:** first-order Trotter with these exact parameters replicates the sources; choosing a smaller $\Delta t$ would reduce Trotter error but change the Floquet-like dynamics the sources actually study, destroying comparability.

## 7. Circuit construction

Per Trotter step, following the Paper's Fig. 1 and its caption exactly (gate conventions $R_X(\theta) = e^{-i\theta X/2}$, $R_Z(\theta) = e^{-i\theta Z/2}$, $R_{ZZ}(\theta) = e^{-i\theta Z_i Z_{i+1}/2}$):

- $e^{-iH_X\Delta t}$: one layer of $R_X(\theta^X_i)$ with $\theta^X_i = 2\Omega\Delta t$ on every site $i = 1,\dots,L$.
- $e^{-iH_Z\Delta t}$: one layer of $R_Z(\theta^Z_i)$ with $\theta^Z_i = -4V\Delta t$ for bulk sites $i = 2,\dots,L-1$ and $\theta^Z_1 = \theta^Z_L = -2V\Delta t$ at the boundaries (Paper, Fig. 1 caption).
- $e^{-iH_{ZZ}\Delta t}$: $R_{ZZ}(\theta^{ZZ}_i)$ with $\theta^{ZZ}_i = 2V\Delta t$ on every bond $(i, i+1)$, $i = 1,\dots,L-1$; bonds are scheduled in two layers (even bonds, then odd bonds) since $R_{ZZ}$ gates on disjoint bonds commute.

**Target basis gate set:** $\{rz, sx, x, cx\}$ — the standard IBM Falcon/Eagle basis. Each $R_{ZZ}(\theta)$ decomposes into the standard two-CNOT form $\mathrm{CNOT}_{i,i+1}\, R_Z(\theta)_{i+1}\, \mathrm{CNOT}_{i,i+1}$ (Paper, Sec. II and Fig. 2(a) describe exactly this two-CNOT compilation; the pulse-level scaled-$R_{ZX}$ alternative is hardware-only and out of scope, see §14). $R_X(\theta)$ compiles to $R_Z$/$SX$ sequences.

**Gate counts and depth** as functions of chain length $L$ and step count $n$:

| Quantity | Per Trotter step | Total after $n$ steps |
|---|---|---|
| CNOT ($cx$) count | $2(L-1)$ | $2(L-1)\,n$ |
| $R_{ZZ}$ blocks | $L-1$ | $(L-1)\,n$ |
| Single-qubit rotations (pre-transpile) | $2L$ ($R_X$ + $R_Z$ layers) | $2Ln$ (+ $\lfloor L/2 \rfloor$ prep $X$ gates) |
| Circuit depth (layers, pre-transpile) | $\approx 8$ (1 $R_X$ + 1 $R_Z$ + 2×(CNOT, $R_Z$, CNOT)) | $\approx 8n + 1$ |

**Minimal experiment:** $L = 6$ qubits (the Example's system size), so 10 CNOTs per step and **400 CNOTs at $n = 40$**.

**JUSTIFICATION:** $L=6$ matches the Example, keeps the density-matrix simulation trivial ($2^6 = 64$-dimensional, $64\times 64$ density matrix), and is large enough for the scar oscillation to be visible (the Example demonstrates this at $L=6$). Transpilation uses `optimization_level=0` (as the Example does): this is **load-bearing**, because ZNE folding inserts $G G^\dagger G$ sequences that an optimizing transpiler would cancel, silently undoing the noise scaling. Folding is applied to the already-transpiled circuit and the executor must never re-optimize it.

## 8. Noise model

**Channel type:** local depolarizing channels attached to gates, built with `qiskit_aer.noise.depolarizing_error(param, num_qubits)` and `NoiseModel.add_all_qubit_quantum_error(error, instructions)` (both verified in the Qiskit Aer 0.17.1 API docs; `depolarizing_error` returns a `QuantumError` with depolarizing parameter $0 \le \lambda_{\text{dep}} \le 4^n/(4^n-1)$).

**Exact specification:**

| Gates | Channel | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| $sx$, $sxdg$, $x$ (all qubits) | 1-qubit depolarizing | $p_1 = 10^{-3}$ |
| $cx$ (all qubit pairs) | 2-qubit depolarizing | $p_2 = 10^{-2}$ |
| $rz$ | none (noiseless) | — |
| State prep / measurement | none | — |

$sxdg$ is covered even though our own transpilation to $\{rz, sx, x, cx\}$ never emits it: Aer noise is keyed by **instruction name**, and Qiskit's `SXGate.inverse()` "is always a `SXdgGate`" (Qiskit API docs), so any tool that inserts gate inverses — ZNE folding in particular, which builds $G^\dagger G$ via `inverse(op)` (mitiq v1.0.0 `folding.py`) — could otherwise smuggle a *noiseless* single-qubit instruction into a "noisy" circuit. The folding policy of §10 is restricted so this path is never exercised, but the channel is attached anyway as defense in depth, and test T6 (§17) verifies that every instruction appearing in any executed circuit is either $rz$ or carries a noise channel.

**JUSTIFICATION (realism vs tractability):** two-tier rates mirror the hardware regime the Paper reports: "single-qubit gates have error rates of $\sim 10^{-4}$–$10^{-3}$, while CNOT gates … have error rates of $\sim 5\times 10^{-3}$ to $1.5\times 10^{-2}$" (Paper, Sec. III); $p_2 = 10^{-2}$ sits inside the Paper's measured CNOT range (average two-qubit error $1.8\times 10^{-2}$ on `ibmq_guadalupe`, $1.6 \times 10^{-2}$ on `ibmq_toronto`). $rz$ is noiseless because it is a virtual (frame-change) gate on IBM devices. Depolarizing noise is the tractable idealization under which ZNE's assumptions are cleanest (Paper, Sec. III: ZNE "is best justified in the case where gate errors result in a stochastic quantum channel"); coherent errors, $T_1/T_2$, and readout error are deliberately excluded (threat: §18). **Deviation from the Example, flagged:** the Example uses `initialized_depolarizing_noise(noise_level=0.02)`, which applies the *same* 0.02 depolarizing error to `u1, u2, u3` and `cx` alike (verified from the mitiq v1.0.0 source of `mitiq.interface.mitiq_qiskit.qiskit_utils`). We deviate because equal single- and two-qubit rates are unphysical and because 0.02 on every gate drives the $n\gtrsim 20$ circuits so deep into depolarization that all scale factors measure nearly zero signal.

**DECISION — deterministic density-matrix simulation is primary.** The primary run uses `AerSimulator(method="density_matrix")` ("density_matrix" is a documented AerSimulator method) with a `save_expectation_value` instruction, giving the *exact* noisy expectation value $\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_\lambda Z_\pi]/L$ with zero shot noise. **JUSTIFICATION:** the scientific question (§1) is about ZNE's systematic performance; shot noise only blurs the measurement, and removing it makes every reported number a deterministic function of the specification. A **secondary shot-based run** reproduces the realistic setting: 8192 shots per circuit (the value used by both the Paper, Sec. III, and the Example), `seed_simulator` fixed per circuit (documented Aer run option; deterministic given the seed), seed schedule in §16.

**Folding stochasticity (addressed head-on):** `fold_gates_at_random` **is seedable** — its v1.0.0 signature is `fold_gates_at_random(circuit, scale_factor, seed: int | None = None, **kwargs)` (verified from source). `execute_with_zne` does not thread a seed through `scale_noise`, so the seed is bound with `functools.partial(fold_gates_at_random, seed=s)`. A single folding configuration is still one random draw, so the primary experiment **averages over $N_{\text{fold}} = 8$ seeded repetitions**: for each seed $s \in \{1000,\dots,1007\}$, run the full ZNE estimate with that folding seed, then average the 8 ZNE estimates. For the linear factory this equals extrapolating the seed-averaged expectation values, since a least-squares linear fit is linear in the data. The seed-ensemble statistics (central value, band, and propagation into the error metrics) are pre-registered in §13.

## 9. ZNE scale factors

$$\lambda \in \{1.0,\; 1.5,\; 2.0\}.$$

**JUSTIFICATION:** these are exactly the scale factors the Paper uses for its ZNE ("linear ZNE with scale factors $\lambda \in \{1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$", Fig. 4 caption and Sec. IV.A). Choosing the Paper's values over the Example's $\{1.0, 1.25, 1.5\}$ maximizes comparability with the peer-reviewed source and gives a wider lever arm for the fit. Count (3): the minimum that lets the linear-fit residual at the middle point diagnose curvature, while keeping circuit overhead at $3\times$ the base cost. **Bias/variance tradeoff:** under the heuristic global-depolarizing picture of §11 the signal decays exponentially in the scaled noise exposure (a heuristic, not a theorem — §11), so larger $\lambda$ (i) pushes points onto the flat, near-zero tail where the *relative* effect of shot noise blows up (variance, relevant to the secondary shot run), and (ii) increases the curvature of the sampled segment of the exponential, which is precisely the systematic bias of a linear extrapolant (quantified in §11). Small maximum $\lambda = 2$ bounds both. Non-integer $\lambda = 1.5$ is supported by the chosen folding function (§10).

## 10. Noise-scaling method

**Chosen: random gate folding restricted to two-qubit gates**: `mitiq.zne.scaling.fold_gates_at_random` applied to the transpiled circuit with bound seeds (§8) **and** an explicit fidelity dictionary

```python
fidelities = {"single": 1.0, "double": 0.99}
```

Verified v1.0.0 semantics (from `folding.py` source): `fidelities` keys may name gate classes (`"single"`, `"double"`, `"triple"`) or specific gates; folding weights are `1 - fidelity`; and "Gates with zero weight are assumed to be ideal and are not folded." Setting `"single": 1.0` therefore **excludes every single-qubit gate from folding**; with only one foldable class remaining ($cx$), the numeric value 0.99 is arbitrary (it must merely lie in the required interval $(0,1]$). This reproduces the Paper's actual method: it performs "gate folding $G \to GG^\dagger G$ on randomly chosen **two-qubit gates** throughout the circuit. This results in a noise scale factor $\lambda$ that can be noninteger" (Sec. III). It also makes every folded gate self-inverse: $cx^\dagger = cx$, so the `inverse(op)` that mitiq inserts (v1.0.0 `folding.py`: `folded_circuit.append([op] + num_folds * [inverse(op), op])`) is again a $cx$ carrying the same $p_2$ channel — no inverse-instruction leakage (§8, finding addressed there for the general case).

**Default behavior explicitly rejected.** Without `fidelities`, v1.0.0 folds *all* gates with the near-uniform default weight `_default_weight(op) = 0.99 ** len(op.qubits)` (i.e. 0.99 for 1-qubit, 0.9801 for 2-qubit gates — verified from source). Under that default, noiseless $rz$ gates receive nonzero folding weight while the actual rates $p_1, p_2$ are never communicated to Mitiq, so the folded circuit's *noise* scale would bear no controlled relation to the requested $\lambda$. The explicit dictionary above is therefore mandatory, not optional.

**The noise scaling is approximate, and is checked empirically — not "by construction".** Three approximations remain even with restricted folding, and all are recorded rather than assumed away:

1. *Realized vs requested scale.* Mitiq's own docstring promises only that the folded circuit has a gate count "approximately equal to `scale_factor * n`". M2 therefore records, for every executed circuit (each $(n, \lambda, s)$ triple), the per-instruction-name gate counts of the folded circuit, defining the **realized two-qubit scale** $\lambda_r = N_{cx}^{\text{folded}} / N_{cx}^{\text{base}}$ alongside the nominal $\lambda$. $\lambda_r$ is *measured from the folded circuit*, never inferred from mitiq's internal rounding rule (which this design does not assume).
2. *Folding granularity at small circuits.* Each fold of one $cx$ appends exactly two gates ($G \to G\,G^\dagger G$), so on a base circuit with $N_{cx} = 2(L-1)n = 10n$ two-qubit gates the achievable scales are **quantized**: $\lambda_r \in \{1 + m/(5n) : m = 0, 1, 2, \dots\}$. The achievable values nearest the nominal targets:

   | $n$ | grid spacing $1/(5n)$ | $\lambda = 1.0$ | $\lambda = 1.5$ | $\lambda = 2.0$ |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | 0.2 | 1.0 exact | **not achievable** — grid is $\{1.0, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8, 2.0\}$; realized 1.4 or 1.6 | 2.0 exact |
   | 2 | 0.1 | 1.0 exact | 1.5 exact | 2.0 exact |
   | 5 | 0.04 | 1.0 exact | not exact; realized 1.48 or 1.52 | 2.0 exact |
   | 10 | 0.02 | 1.0 exact | 1.5 exact | 2.0 exact |
   | 20 | 0.01 | 1.0 exact | 1.5 exact | 2.0 exact |
   | 40 | 0.005 | 1.0 exact | 1.5 exact | 2.0 exact |

   In general $\lambda = 1.0$ and $\lambda = 2.0$ ($m = 5n$) are exactly achievable at every $n$, while $\lambda = 1.5$ ($m = 2.5n$) is exactly achievable only at **even** $n$: every odd step $n = 1, 3, \dots, 39$ realizes $1.5 \pm$ at most $1/(10n)$ instead (worst at $n = 1$: off by up to $0.1$; already $\le 0.02$ from $n = 5$). **Pre-registered choice, stated before any results exist: the primary analysis regresses on the *realized* abscissas $\lambda_r$**, not on nominal $\lambda$. Justification: $\lambda_r$ is exactly known and deterministic (seeded folding, counted gates), so regressing on it removes a known, avoidable errors-in-variables misspecification; the protocol remains source-faithful in method (same folding, same nominal targets, same linear model) — only the regression abscissa is corrected to the truth, and the two regressions coincide identically at every step where the nominal grid is achievable (all even $n$, and $\lambda \in \{1, 2\}$ always). The **nominal-$\lambda$ regression is computed and reported alongside** as the source-faithful comparison. In M2 this is implemented by folding the seeded circuits explicitly, counting gates, and fitting the same linear model to the recorded $(\lambda_r, E)$ pairs (via the factory or the equivalent pre-registered OLS of §11); the `execute_with_zne` path in §15 illustrates the nominal-$\lambda$ comparison arm.
3. *Unscaled single-qubit noise.* Folding only $cx$ leaves the single-qubit depolarizing exposure fixed. Define the effective total-noise scale with the §11 exposure weights $w_k \equiv \Gamma_k = N_k\,\gamma_k$ (where $\gamma_k = -\ln(1-p_k)$ and $N_k$ counts the noisy $k$-qubit gate applications in the base circuit at the given $n$):
   $$
   \lambda_{\text{eff}} \;=\; \frac{\Gamma_2\,\lambda_r + \Gamma_1}{\Gamma_2 + \Gamma_1} \;\le\; \lambda_r,
   \qquad \text{with equality at } \lambda_r = 1 .
   $$
   Under the §8 rates the $cx$ exposure dominates ($2(L-1)$ gates at $p_2 = 10^{-2}$ per step versus single-qubit gates at $p_1 = 10^{-3}$; the exact split is fixed by the recorded transpiled circuit in M2), so the gap between $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ and $\lambda_r$ is modest, signed, and documented. **The $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ arm is more than a robustness check, and the reason is one line of algebra:** by construction $(\Gamma_1 + \Gamma_2)\,\lambda_{\text{eff}} = \Gamma_2\lambda_r + \Gamma_1$ *exactly*, so the §11 heuristic curve $E \approx E_0\,e^{-(\Gamma_2\lambda_r + \Gamma_1)}$ is a **pure exponential in $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ with intercept $E_0$**: extrapolating to $\lambda_{\text{eff}} = 0$ targets the full noiseless value, floor included. The single-qubit noise floor of §11 is therefore a property of the *abscissa*, not of ZNE as such — it afflicts the nominal-$\lambda$ and $\lambda_r$ regressions (whose heuristic intercept is $E_0 e^{-\Gamma_1}$) but not the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ regression. This makes the pre-registered $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ arm scientifically interesting in its own right: comparing its empirical intercept against the $\lambda_r$ arm's measures how real the floor is. It nonetheless stays a *diagnostic* rather than the primary, for a pre-registered reason: $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ is a **model-derived** abscissa — it depends on the assumed rates $p_k$ and on the global-depolarizing heuristic itself (its ability to reach $E_0$ holds only insofar as that heuristic holds) — whereas $\lambda_r$ is a model-free measured gate count. A primary analysis should not carry a model-derived design matrix; the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ arm is computed per seed and reported next to the primary $\lambda_r$ regression and the nominal-$\lambda$ comparison arm.

**Non-integer scale factors are supported:** the v1.0.0 docstring states the scale factor may be "Any real number >= 1" (verified from source). Alternatives considered: `fold_global` (deterministic, also any $\lambda \ge 1$) is attractive for reproducibility but deviates from both sources and concentrates folded noise at the circuit boundary; `insert_id_layers` scales idle time, which is meaningless under our gate-attached noise model (no idle noise present); layerwise folding (`layer_folding`) adds structure the sources don't use. Seeded, fidelity-restricted random folding recovers determinism (§8, §16) while matching the Paper's method. All module paths verified from `mitiq/zne/scaling/__init__.py` at tag v1.0.0: `fold_all`, `fold_gates_at_random`, `fold_global` (from `.folding`), `layer_folding`, `get_layer_folding` (from `.layer_scaling`), `scale_parameters`, `compute_parameter_variance` (from `.parameter`), `insert_id_layers` (from `.identity_insertion`).

## 11. Extrapolation model

**Chosen: `mitiq.zne.inference.LinearFactory(scale_factors=[1.0, 1.5, 2.0])`** as the primary, pre-registered extrapolation, with **`ExpFactory(scale_factors=[1.0, 1.5, 2.0], asymptote=0.0)`** as a pre-registered secondary computed from the *same* data. (Constructor signatures verified from v1.0.0 `mitiq/zne/inference.py`: `LinearFactory(scale_factors, shot_list=None)`; `ExpFactory(scale_factors, asymptote=None, avoid_log=False, shot_list=None)`.)

**The expected noise curve, head-on — as a heuristic, not a theorem.** Aer's depolarizing channel is defined *locally*: each $k$-qubit instance acts as $\mathcal{E}(\rho) = (1-p)\rho + p\,\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)\, I/2^k$ (Aer `depolarizing_error` docs). Interleaved local channels on different supports do **not** in general contract an arbitrary many-body observable by a single scalar factor: the exact response is a sum of decay modes, and near times where the noiseless value $E_0(n) \approx 0$ the noisy value need not be proportional to $E_0$ at all. What tracelessness of $Z_\pi$ *does* guarantee is only the endpoint: $\langle Z_\pi \rangle = 0$ in the maximally mixed state $I/2^L$; it proves neither a single-exponential shape nor that the late-time state is exactly maximally mixed. As a **heuristic diagnostic** we nonetheless adopt the global-depolarizing approximation, under which the noisy expectation at scale factor $\lambda$ is

$$
E(\lambda) \;\approx\; E_0\, e^{-\left(\Gamma_2\,\lambda \;+\; \Gamma_1\right)}, \qquad
\Gamma_2 = N_2\,\gamma_2, \quad \Gamma_1 = N_1\,\gamma_1, \qquad
\gamma_k \equiv -\ln(1 - p_k),
$$

where $N_2 = 2(L-1)\,n$ is the **total** number of noisy two-qubit gate applications in the whole circuit at $\lambda = 1$ and $N_1$ is the **total** number of noisy single-qubit gate applications ($sx$/$x$ from the transpiled Trotter layers *plus* the $\lfloor L/2\rfloor$ state-preparation $X$ gates; the exact $N_1$ is fixed by the recorded transpiled circuit in M2). **Only the two-qubit term carries $\lambda$**, because the §10 folding policy folds $cx$ gates exclusively: the single-qubit exposure $\Gamma_1$ is a $\lambda$-independent offset that folding never touches. The consequence must be stated plainly, with its scope: even a *perfect* extrapolation of this heuristic curve to zero **in the nominal-$\lambda$ or $\lambda_r$ abscissa** recovers $E_0\,e^{-\Gamma_1}$, **not** $E_0$ — in those abscissas the $\lambda$-independent noise floor is invisible to any extrapolant. The exception is the exposure-based $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ abscissa of §10: since $(\Gamma_1+\Gamma_2)\lambda_{\text{eff}} = \Gamma_2\lambda_r + \Gamma_1$ exactly, the heuristic curve is a pure exponential in $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ with intercept $E_0$, so the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ diagnostic arm is the one regression that targets $E_0$ (under the heuristic, and only under it — §10). This is an honest limitation of the design, accepted deliberately (folding two-qubit gates only is the Paper's method, and the $cx$ exposure dominates under the §8 rates); its magnitude grows linearly in $n$: with $N_1$ of order 10–15 per step (exact value fixed by the recorded transpiled circuit), $\Gamma_1 \approx N_1 p_1$ reaches $O(0.5)$ by $n = 40$, i.e. a several-tens-of-percent attenuation at late steps *under the heuristic* that neither the nominal-$\lambda$ nor the $\lambda_r$ extrapolation can undo (the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ diagnostic arm is the exception — §10). Under this heuristic the curve in $\lambda$ is exponential, not linear, so a linear fit is *misspecified in principle*; how badly, in practice, is quantified below and measured empirically (that is the point of running both factories). The linear fit is adopted as primary for three reasons:

1. **Source fidelity.** The Paper's many-body ZNE "is performed with a linear extrapolation to $\lambda = 0$ for each Trotter step" (Sec. IV.A; Fig. 4 caption: "linear ZNE with scale factors $\lambda \in \{1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$"), and the Example's final time-evolution protocol uses `LinearFactory`. Using the same estimator makes our controlled measurement a statement about the sources' actual protocol. **Attribution note (correcting an earlier draft):** the Paper *does* also fit a linear model $F_0 - \epsilon\lambda$ and calls it "justified under the assumptions that $\epsilon$ is small and that $G$ and $G^\dagger$ have identical error rates" — but that passage belongs to its quantum-process-tomography benchmark of a **single $R_{ZZ}(\theta)$ gate** at $\lambda \in \{1, 3, 5\}$ on `ibmq_casablanca` (Sec. II), with both stated assumptions. It is a gate-fidelity fit, not a derivation of linearity for the many-body $\langle Z_\pi\rangle$ response, and it is not used as theoretical support here.
2. **Controlled bias over the chosen range (heuristic model).** The chosen estimator is `LinearFactory`, an ordinary degree-one least-squares fit; for the design points $\lambda \in \{1, 1.5, 2\}$ its zero-noise intercept is the exact linear combination
   $$
   \widehat{E}(0) \;=\; \tfrac{11}{6}\,E(1) \;+\; \tfrac13\,E(1.5) \;-\; \tfrac76\,E(2).
   $$
   Substituting the heuristic curve $E(\lambda) = E_0 e^{-\Gamma_1} e^{-\Gamma_2\lambda}$ gives
   $$
   \widehat{E}(0) \;=\; E_0 e^{-\Gamma_1}\!\left[\tfrac{11}{6}e^{-\Gamma_2} + \tfrac13 e^{-3\Gamma_2/2} - \tfrac76 e^{-2\Gamma_2}\right]
   \;=\; E_0 e^{-\Gamma_1}\!\left[1 - \tfrac{25}{24}\,\Gamma_2^2 + O(\Gamma_2^3)\right].
   $$
   The $\tfrac{25}{24}$ coefficient and its derivation are unchanged from the single-exponent form because the unscaled factor $e^{-\Gamma_1}$ multiplies all three data points equally and the OLS estimator is linear in the data — only the interpretation shifts: the curvature argument is $\Gamma_2$ (the scaled two-qubit exposure), and the quantity the fit converges to is the ZNE-accessible target $E_0 e^{-\Gamma_1}$, not $E_0$. Two distinct systematic effects therefore coexist: (i) the multiplicative floor $e^{-\Gamma_1}$, a property of the folding policy together with the regression abscissa, present for any extrapolant in the nominal-$\lambda$ or $\lambda_r$ abscissa (but not in $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ — §10); (ii) the OLS curvature bias $-\tfrac{25}{24}\Gamma_2^2$, a property of the linear model, directed **toward zero** relative to that floor. Effect (ii) is a *conservative* failure mode (it can make ZNE look worse, not spuriously better) — acceptable for a study whose pass/fail metric must be robust against flattering artifacts. These weights are exact for the abscissas $\{1, 1.5, 2\}$; at steps where the primary regression uses realized abscissas $\lambda_r \ne \lambda$ (§10, odd $n$), the OLS weights are recomputed from the recorded abscissas — the qualitative conclusions are unchanged. All statements inherit the heuristic status of the exponential model; the empirical bias is what §13 actually measures.
3. **Robustness.** With a known asymptote, `ExpFactory` fits two exponential parameters to the three points by a *weighted log-linear least-squares regression* on the asymptote-shifted values (so it is a regression, not an exactly-determined fit). Verified v1.0.0 behavior (`inference.py` source): the shifted values are computed as `shifted_y = [max(sign * (y - asymptote), eps) for y in exp_values]` with `eps = 1.0e-6` — i.e. Mitiq infers an overall sign and **clamps** wrong-sign or near-zero shifted values to $10^{-6}$ before taking the logarithm. It therefore does not diverge when a point crosses zero, but the clamping can introduce severe, silent bias exactly in the late-$n$, heavily depolarized regime where expectation values fluctuate around 0. The linear estimator has no such regime change and degrades gracefully there.

The secondary `ExpFactory(asymptote=0.0)` **matches the heuristic exponential form above** (the fixed asymptote is the maximally-mixed value 0 from tracelessness); it is *not* guaranteed to be the true response of the local-channel model, so it serves as a model-sensitivity probe rather than ground truth. Reporting both factories quantifies the model-dependence of the mitigated value empirically at every Trotter step. **Pre-registered handling of clamped fits and ensemble aggregation:** for every step and seed, M2 records whether any shifted value triggered the `eps` clamp (wrong sign or magnitude $\le 10^{-6}$); clamped log-mode estimates are flagged **invalid** and are never averaged together with unclamped ones. The per-step secondary estimate is kept **homogeneous across the seed ensemble**: if *no* seed at step $n$ is flagged, it is the 8-seed mean of the log-mode fits; if *any* seed at step $n$ is flagged, **all eight seeds** at that step are rerun with `avoid_log=True` (direct nonlinear fit of the exponential ansatz — v1.0.0 `inference.py`) and the secondary estimate is the 8-seed mean of the avoid-log fits, with the step marked as avoid-log mode in the results. Uncertainty bands for the secondary follow the same `ddof=1` rules as §13, computed within the single mode actually used. All per-seed values in both modes are recorded regardless. **What the linear choice introduces, stated for the record:** under the heuristic model, a systematic shrinkage of $|\langle Z_\pi\rangle|$ toward zero with leading coefficient $\tfrac{25}{24}\,E_0 e^{-\Gamma_1}\Gamma_2^2$, growing quadratically with the scaled circuit depth, and expected to dominate the residual mitigated error at late $n$ — on top of the $\lambda$-independent floor $e^{-\Gamma_1}$ that no extrapolant in the nominal-$\lambda$ or $\lambda_r$ abscissa removes (§10's $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ arm is the heuristic exception). Richardson extrapolation (`RichardsonFactory`, the v1.0.0 default when `factory=None`, with default scale factors $[1,2,3]$ — verified from source) is rejected for the primary protocol: through 3 points it is a quadratic extrapolant whose higher-order coefficients amplify noise/scatter, and neither source uses it **for its final reported scar-dynamics protocol** (the Example does run the default Richardson estimator once, as an initial single-circuit API demonstration, before selecting linear extrapolation for its time-evolution figure — see §14(b)).

## 12. Noiseless reference

Two distinct references, both recorded:

1. **Primary: exact statevector of the Trotterized circuit.** The same transpiled circuit executed with noise-free `AerSimulator(method="statevector")` (or `Statevector` evolution), giving $E_0(n) = \langle Z_\pi(n\Delta t)\rangle_{\text{Trotter}}/L$. **This is the primary comparison target** because it is the noiseless value of the identical circuit — the only fair yardstick for mitigation error, against which residual unextrapolated noise (the single-qubit floor of §10–§11) correctly counts as error. Note the distinction: $E_0$ is the *evaluation target*, not a value the implemented extrapolation is claimed to reach — under the §11 heuristic the primary $\lambda_r$ regression converges to $E_0 e^{-\Gamma_1}$, not $E_0$ (only the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ diagnostic arm targets $E_0$, §10). This mirrors the Paper, which benchmarks QPU data against "a noiseless statevector simulator" via its accumulated-error metric $D(t)$ (Paper Eq. (3.1)).
2. **Secondary: exact continuous-time evolution.** $\langle Z_2 | e^{iHt} Z_\pi e^{-iHt} | Z_2\rangle / L$ computed by exact diagonalization / `scipy.linalg.expm` of the $2^L$-dimensional $H$ of §3. The gap between reference 2 and reference 1 **is** the Trotter error, plotted as its own labelled curve so discretization error and hardware-noise error are never conflated (§6).

These are different quantities at $\Delta t = 1$ and the design treats them as such; conflating them would let Trotter error contaminate the ZNE metrics.

## 13. Comparison metrics

Let $E_0(n)$ be the noiseless Trotterized reference, $E_{\text{noisy}}(n)$ the unmitigated noisy value ($\lambda = 1$), and $E_{\text{ZNE}}(n)$ the seed-averaged ZNE estimate, all for $\langle Z_\pi\rangle/L$ at step $n$.

- Unmitigated error: $\;\varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n) = \bigl| E_{\text{noisy}}(n) - E_0(n) \bigr|$.
- Mitigated error: $\;\varepsilon_{\text{m}}(n) = \bigl| E_{\text{ZNE}}(n) - E_0(n) \bigr|$.
- Per-step improvement factor: $\;\mathrm{IF}(n) = \varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n) / \varepsilon_{\text{m}}(n)$ (numerator: unmitigated error; denominator: mitigated error; $\mathrm{IF} > 1$ means ZNE helped).
- **Baseline-relevance filter (this is *not* a denominator guard):** $\mathrm{IF}(n)$ enters the pass/fail majority test only at steps where $\varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n) \ge \varepsilon_{\min} = 0.01$ — i.e. 1% of the observable's maximum magnitude $|\langle Z_\pi\rangle|/L = 1$ (equivalently 0.5% of the full $[-1,1]$ span). Where the unmitigated error is already below $\varepsilon_{\min}$ there is nothing meaningful to mitigate; such points are tabulated but excluded from the majority test. This filter constrains the *numerator*, not the denominator, and by itself prevents no division pathology.
- **Denominator handling (pre-registered):** with $\delta = 10^{-9}$ for the deterministic density-matrix pipeline and $\delta = 10^{-3}$ for the shot-based pipeline — the latter chosen **operationally as a resolution threshold**: differences below $10^{-3}$ are not meaningfully resolved by this protocol at $8192$ shots $\times$ 8 seeds (per-circuit binomial standard error of order $10^{-2}$, of order $10^{-3}$ after seed averaging in the best case; a bounded observable's standard error has an *upper bound*, not a universal positive floor, and can approach zero for near-deterministic outcomes). The shot-pipeline verdict uses seeded point estimates throughout; no significance test is performed or implied. If $\varepsilon_{\text{m}}(n) \le \delta$ — including exactly zero — then $\mathrm{IF}(n)$ is not reported as a point value but as the flagged lower bound $\mathrm{IF}(n) \ge \varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n)/\delta$; for the majority test such a step counts as $\mathrm{IF}(n) > 1$ iff $\varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n)/\delta > 1$. No non-finite value ever enters any table or aggregate.
- Aggregate over the window: root-mean-square errors $\mathrm{RMS}_{\text{u}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{40}\sum_{n=1}^{40}\varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n)^2}$, $\mathrm{RMS}_{\text{m}}$ analogously, and the **global improvement factor** $\mathrm{GIF} = \mathrm{RMS}_{\text{u}} / \mathrm{RMS}_{\text{m}}$ (ratio of RMS aggregates — no per-step division). **The RMS sums and GIF always run over all 40 steps; the baseline-relevance filter does not touch them.**
- **Saturation at late steps (interpretive, pre-registered):** the noise model attenuates the noisy and mitigated values toward zero while the noiseless reference $E_0(n)$ remains an $O(1)$ scar oscillation, so at late $n$ both $\varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n)$ and $\varepsilon_{\text{m}}(n)$ saturate toward $|E_0(n)|$ and $\mathrm{IF}(n) \to 1$. In that regime the metrics correctly report "no improvement" but cannot distinguish a ZNE failure from the absence of any remaining signal to mitigate (§18(2)); the per-step tables keep this regime visible rather than letting the 40-step aggregates average it away.
- **Uncertainty statistics (pre-registered, resolving what "spread" means):** for each step $n$, the per-seed ZNE estimates $E^{(s)}_{\text{ZNE}}(n)$, $s = 1,\dots,8$, give the central value $\bar E_{\text{ZNE}}(n)$ = sample mean; the reported band is $\pm$ one sample standard deviation with `ddof=1`; the standard error $s_n/\sqrt{8}$ is also recorded. The central mitigated error is $\varepsilon_{\text{m}}(n) = |\bar E_{\text{ZNE}}(n) - E_0(n)|$; its band is **not** obtained by linearized propagation through the absolute value — instead the per-seed errors $\varepsilon^{(s)}_{\text{m}}(n) = |E^{(s)}_{\text{ZNE}}(n) - E_0(n)|$ are computed directly and their `ddof=1` sample standard deviation is the band. **Unmitigated baseline:** in the density-matrix pipeline the $\lambda = 1$ value is seed-independent (folding at $\lambda = 1$ returns the circuit unchanged), so $E_{\text{noisy}}(n)$ is a single deterministic number; in the shot-based pipeline it is defined as the mean of the eight independently seeded $\lambda = 1$ executions, with its `ddof=1` standard deviation reported alongside.
- Reporting across Trotter steps: all quantities tabulated at every $n$ and plotted vs $Vt = n$.

**Pass/fail criterion for the minimal experiment (pre-registered):** let $m$ be the number of steps passing the baseline-relevance filter ("reportable steps"). The run *passes* if $\mathrm{GIF} > 1$ over the full window $n = 1,\dots,40$ **and** $\mathrm{IF}(n) > 1$ on strictly more than $m/2$ of the reportable steps (this resolves odd $m$ without ties; for even $m$ an exact tie fails). **Edge case $m = 0$** (noise too weak for mitigation to be meaningful anywhere): the majority clause is vacuous, the verdict is determined by the GIF clause alone, and the report must flag $m = 0$ prominently. Otherwise it *fails*. **A failure is a valid, reportable scientific outcome:** the result is preserved, published in `results/` exactly as produced, and must not be tuned away by post-hoc adjustment of noise rates, scale factors, seeds, or the extrapolation model. Any parameter change after seeing results starts a new, separately labelled experiment.

## 18. Limitations and threats to validity

1. **Noise-model idealization.** Pure gate-attached depolarizing noise is the friendliest case for ZNE (perfectly stochastic; and under the §10 policy the $cx$ exposure is approximately scaled by folding while the single-qubit share is not scaled at all — see §10–§11). Real devices add coherent errors, crosstalk, $T_1/T_2$, non-Markovianity, and readout error, under which the folding–noise relationship degrades further still. Results here bound nothing about hardware (§14d).
2. **Extrapolation-model misspecification.** Under the heuristic exponential model the linear primary has an $O(\Gamma_2^2)$ shrinkage bias and a $\lambda$-independent single-qubit noise floor $e^{-\Gamma_1}$ that neither the nominal-$\lambda$ nor the $\lambda_r$ regression removes (the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ arm targets $E_0$ under the heuristic only — §10, §11), and the true local-channel response need not be that exponential at all; at late Trotter steps (total exposure $\Gamma_1 + \lambda\Gamma_2 \gtrsim 1$, hundreds of noisy CNOTs) the exponential has decayed so far that no extrapolation from $\lambda \ge 1$ recovers *usable signal from the realized data, whatever the abscissa*. What degrades, precisely: the noiseless reference $E_0(n)$ is untouched by the noise model by definition — it remains an $O(1)$, weakly damped scar oscillation across the whole window — while the noisy and mitigated values are attenuated toward zero. Both error metrics therefore **saturate** rather than shrink: $\varepsilon_{\text{u}}(n)$ and $\varepsilon_{\text{m}}(n)$ each approach $|E_0(n)|$ — which is $O(1)$ wherever the oscillation is away from a zero crossing, so the late-$n$ failure mode is *large*, saturated absolute errors — and $\mathrm{IF}(n) \to 1$ with GIF pulled toward 1: ZNE neither helps nor hurts because no signal remains in the data to extrapolate. A separate, narrower degeneracy arises only at steps where $E_0(n)$ itself sits near a zero crossing: there both errors are genuinely small and relative comparisons degenerate — exactly the steps the §13 baseline-relevance filter ($\varepsilon_{\text{u}} \ge 0.01$) already excludes from the majority test. In the shot pipeline specifically, relative shot noise diverges as $|\langle Z_\pi\rangle|/L \to 0$ (the absolute binomial standard error stays of order $1/\sqrt{N_{\text{shots}}}$, so the ratio blows up); and the one estimator that genuinely becomes ill-conditioned is the log-linearized exponential secondary, whose asymptote-shifted values approach zero and hit the $10^{-6}$ clamp — exactly the regime §11 already flags as invalid. The linear primary does **not** become ill-conditioned: its design-matrix conditioning is fixed entirely by the abscissas ($\{1, 1.5, 2\}$ or the realized $\lambda_r$) and is independent of the collapsing $y$-values, and the deterministic density-matrix pipeline has no sampling variance at all. This is a signal-to-noise limit on realized data, distinct from the algebraic floor of §11 — the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ intercept is exact only in the noiseless heuristic, which says nothing about fitting three near-identical, near-zero points in practice. ZNE is expected to fail there, and the design must report that regime honestly rather than truncate it.
3. **Folding–noise-model calibration.** Any optimization pass after folding can cancel $G^\dagger G$ pairs and silently break noise scaling (guarded by `optimization_level=0` and tests T5–T6); the achievable fold grid is quantized, so nominal $\lambda = 1.5$ is unrealizable at odd $n$ (handled by the pre-registered $\lambda_r$ regression, §10); and the single-qubit noise share is not scaled at all under the §10 policy, so the effective scale $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ sits at or below $\lambda_r$ — the resulting $\lambda$-independent floor is structurally unremovable in the nominal-$\lambda$ and $\lambda_r$ abscissas (§11; the $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ arm escapes it only insofar as the §11 heuristic and the assumed rates hold, §10) and is the residual calibration limit of the primary design.
4. **Single small system.** $L=6$, one initial state, one observable, one $(p_1, p_2)$ pair. Conclusions do not transfer to larger $L$ (error grows with gate count), other observables, or other noise strengths without new runs.
5. **Trotter/Floquet regime.** At $\Delta t = 1$ the circuit is better viewed as a Floquet system (Paper, Sec. II); statements about the continuous-time MFIM are limited to the ED reference curve.
6. **Seed sensitivity.** 8 folding seeds estimate the folding-configuration spread coarsely; a pathological seed set could bias $E_{\text{ZNE}}$. Mitigation: report the full per-seed spread, never just the mean.
7. **Version drift in sources.** The Example page ran against an older Mitiq/Qiskit stack (it imports the legacy `QasmSimulator`; current Aer promotes `AerSimulator`, which mitiq v1.0.0's own qiskit utilities import). Numerical agreement with the Example's plotted curves is not a validation target; only qualitative behavior is.
8. **Docs-version mismatch.** Aer API pages fetched are labelled 0.17.1 while PyPI latest is 0.17.2 (§ preamble, **UNVERIFIED** patch-level API stability); the mitiq 1.0.0 changelog mentions a "bug fix for ZNE that had gone unnoticed since its initial implementation" whose exact nature we did not verify (**UNVERIFIED** detail) — M2 must not assume numerical agreement with pre-1.0 Mitiq outputs.
